\begin{abstract}
Hard parameter-sharing U-Nets couple classification and lesion segmentation in resource-constrained medical vision. Recent literature reported near-saturated accuracies ($82\%-90\%$) and Dice scores ($97\%-99\%$) across diverse modalities under standard backbones and static loss weighting. We present a systematic methodological audit and replication across 27 configurations $\times$ 5 folds (135 fold-runs) over four datasets: TCGA-LGG brain-tumor MRI, PANDA prostate biopsies, SIIM-ACR pneumothorax radiographs, and the 19-tissue PanNuke benchmark. Under rigorous 5-fold cross-validation with sample-level and patient-level isolation, published metrics do not reproduce. We investigate three key factors governing this divergence: (1) an empty-mask Dice degeneracy where macroscopic slice averages reward all-empty predictions on negative cases, creating an artificial score floor ($\rho = 77.72\%$ on SIIM) that can conceal severe foreground omission; (2) task-formulation divergence in computational pathology, where the baseline reported $88\%$ accuracy on proxy morphological tissue classes ($52.3\%$ non-cancer stroma/background) whereas authentic clinical ISUP grading on standard thumbnails yields $28.21\%-44.87\%$ top-1 accuracy, reflecting different classification targets; and (3) multi-task optimization dynamics where dynamic gradient balancing (\textsc{GradNorm}) is associated with a substantial drop in validation accuracy ($38.18\% \to 28.21\%$, consistent with destructive gradient interference hypotheses). Furthermore, matched ablations show modest paired classification differences for lateral skip connections ($+0.02$ to $-0.12$ pts), while bypassing Macenko stain normalization consistently improves diagnostic discrimination (+3.81 pts PANDA, +2.75 pts PanNuke). We release our complete benchmark codebase, checkpoints, and evaluation protocols to establish reproducible baselines for multi-task medical vision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep learning systems in digital pathology and radiology are increasingly required to deliver both diagnostic categorization and lesion delineation~\cite{litjens2017survey,campanella2019clinical}. Large visual foundation models (e.g., UNI~\cite{chen2024uni}, CONCH~\cite{lu2024conch}, and Virchow~\cite{vorontsov2024virchow}) show that clinically actionable representations require massive pretraining and self-supervised objectives across gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs). In resource-constrained deployments, hard parameter sharing via an encoder--decoder backbone (a U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015u} with dual prediction heads) remains a lightweight alternative that couples both objectives in a single forward pass.

Recently, Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask} reported that a standard U-Net with lightweight encoders (VGG16~\cite{simonyan2014very} and MobileNetV2~\cite{sandler2018mobilenetv2}) trained with static loss weights ($\lambda_{seg}=5.0, \lambda_{cls}=1.0$) and a high learning rate ($\eta=10^{-3}$) simultaneously solves both classification and segmentation across four distinct medical modalities (Brain MRI, Skin Dermoscopy, Prostate Histopathology, and Chest X-Ray), reporting $82\%-90\%$ classification accuracy and $97\%-99\%$ Dice similarity coefficients.

These figures attach to a recipe --- a stock U-Net under static loss weighting --- not to any architecture- or data-specific innovation, and that is what makes them consequential beyond a single study: the recipe is exactly the lightweight joint pipeline a resource-constrained deployment would copy first. The evaluation literature gives us independent reasons to distrust them. Region-overlap scores degenerate when annotations are sparse or absent~\cite{reinke2024metrics,eelbode2020optimization}; patch-level splits of tiled WSIs leak patient identity~\cite{kapoor2023leakage,zech2018variable}; and joint optimization is documented to interfere with at least one of its tasks~\cite{standley2020tasks,crawshaw2020survey}. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews each strand. The audit that follows asks whether this particular, highly visible instance of the recipe survives the scrutiny those literatures demand.

In this paper, we report a 27-configuration $\times$ 5-fold replication study and systematic methodological audit. Our findings demonstrate that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Task-Formulation Divergence on Prostate Histopathology:} When evaluated under rigorous 5-fold cross-validation on clinical 6-class ordinal ISUP grading (the diagnostic grading standard of the PANDA prostate biopsy benchmark~\cite{bulten2022artificial}), standard CNN models on $128 \times 128$ thumbnails achieve an empirical range of $28.21\%-44.87\%$ top-1 accuracy (fold-mean, 95\% bootstrap CIs $[24.76, 31.47]$ to $[43.93, 45.76]$) across all 12 configurations (Runs 03--04 baseline: $34.51\%$ and $39.91\%$). As documented in Table 2 of Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask}, the published $88\%$ accuracy evaluated proxy morphological tissue categories (Background, Stroma, Benign epithelium, Gleason 3/4/5; where background and stroma alone constituted $52.3\%$ of samples). Because tissue categorization and clinical ISUP grading represent different classification tasks, their accuracies are not directly comparable.
    \item \textbf{The Empty-Mask Dice Metric Degeneracy:} Scoring empty references against empty predictions as $\text{Dice} = 1.0$ introduces a severe score floor on sparse-lesion cohorts. Our SIIM pneumothorax runs converge to all-empty masks on 19 of 20 folds, realizing the all-empty background floor ($77.72\%$ pooled across folds for three configurations; $77.04\%$ for the fourth, matching the negative fraction $\rho$). While the cause of the published 99\% Dice cannot be determined without access to the original predictions and code, our analytical derivation and empirical results prove that macroscopic Dice can conceal severe foreground segmentation failure.
    \item \textbf{Optimization Dynamics in Joint Training:} Matched factorial controls reveal that dynamic gradient balancing is associated with a marked drop in validation accuracy ($38.18\% \to 28.21\%$ Acc, $\Delta = -9.97$ percentage points under matched conditions, consistent with destructive gradient interference hypotheses). Furthermore, lateral skip connections exhibit modest paired classification differences ($+0.02$ percentage points on TCGA, $-0.12$ on PANDA), and omitting Macenko stain normalization consistently yields higher diagnostic discrimination (+3.81 pts on PANDA, +2.75 pts on PanNuke).
\end{enumerate}

To facilitate complete reproducibility, all training scripts, raw JSON summaries for all 27 configurations (135 fold-runs), evaluation checkpoints, and generated figures are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/ApatheticMioz/cancer-pathology-dl}~\cite{ali2026cancerpathologydl}. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the end-to-end pipeline and the three findings it yields.

\section{Related Work: Documented Failure Modes in Multi-Task Medical Segmentation}
\label{sec:related}

None of the three challenges interrogated in this audit is peculiar to the pipeline we examine. Each represents a systemic failure mode that the evaluation, computer vision, and machine-learning literatures have documented independently. This section situates our contribution within those strands; the audit itself instantiates all three within one controlled, artifact-released reproduction of a widely cited joint detection--segmentation recipe.

\emph{Metric degeneracy under sparse annotation.} Scoring an empty prediction against an empty reference as perfect agreement ($\text{Dice} = 1.0$) is a recognized pitfall in biomedical validation: Reinke et al.~\cite{reinke2024metrics} catalogue it among the primary metric traps that distort image analysis benchmarks, and Eelbode et al.~\cite{eelbode2020optimization} demonstrate formally that region-overlap scores behave counter-intuitively when annotations are small or absent. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2024dice} show that region-size bias propagates directly into loss formulations. Slice-averaged Dice on a cohort whose lesion-free fraction approaches $78\%$ sits squarely inside this failure class: Section~\ref{sec:dice} derives the exact decomposition governing what the convention credits.

\emph{Multiscale pathology, resolution limits, and evaluation protocols.} Clinical computational pathology relies on gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs) where diagnostic grades emerge from multiscale tissue architecture~\cite{campanella2019clinical,bulten2022artificial}. Severe spatial downsampling (e.g. compressing biopsy cores to $128 \times 128$ thumbnails) obliterates cellular atypia and gland lumen boundaries, turning cells sub-pixel. Furthermore, patch-level random splits of tiled WSIs risk severe data leakage when tiles from the same patient or biopsy core contaminate both train and test partitions~\cite{kapoor2023leakage,zech2018variable}. When evaluating pathology AI, isolating independent sampling units and aligning tasks with clinical diagnostic targets (rather than proxy tissue textures) is essential for clinical validity.

\emph{Task interference and negative transfer.} Whether classification and segmentation reinforce or degrade one another under a shared encoder depends heavily on task affinity: Standley et al.~\cite{standley2020tasks} observe that naive co-training frequently induces negative transfer across dense and global objectives; Crawshaw's survey~\cite{crawshaw2020survey} collects extensive evidence of gradient conflict; and dynamic gradient balancing~\cite{chen2018gradnorm} is often proposed as a remedy. However, dynamic balancing can destabilize optimization when task losses exhibit disparate convergence dynamics: while Kang et al.~\cite{kang2026gradnorm} report benefits for wound-tissue analysis, our experiments demonstrate that GradNorm forces destructive updates on complex ordinal classification heads.

\emph{Propagation and citation landscape.} These strands converge on the published recipe of Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask}, which reported $97\%-99\%$ Dice alongside $82\%-90\%$ accuracy across four modalities. Because multi-task parameter sharing is widely sought for low-resource clinical deployments, these headline figures have been cited uncritically as evidence of general multi-task efficacy in follow-on oncology work. Notably, recent works such as Al Hassan et al.~\cite{imcet2026leakagesafe} have specifically identified the need for leakage-safe validation and external cohorts when evaluating joint brain tumor models. Supplying a rigorous, empirical, and mathematical quantification of how such numbers arise is the primary focus of this work.

\section{Multi-Task Formulation \& Evaluation Metrics}

\subsection{Architectural Formulation}
Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ denote an input image tile. A shared convolutional encoder $f_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ extracts a bottleneck latent representation $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times d}$. The classification branch applies global average pooling (GAP) followed by a two-layer multilayer perceptron (hidden width 256, dropout $0.5$):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{cls} = \text{Softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{W}_2\,\phi\!\left(\mathbf{W}_1\,\text{GAP}(\mathbf{z}) + \mathbf{b}_1\right) + \mathbf{b}_2\right) \in \Delta^{K-1},
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of diagnostic classes, $\Delta^{K-1}$ denotes the probability simplex, and $\phi(\cdot)$ is the ReLU nonlinearity. The segmentation branch passes $\mathbf{z}$ through a decoder $\text{Dec}_\phi$ with lateral skip connections $\{\mathbf{s}_l\}_{l=1}^L$ from encoder stages, yielding unnormalized pixel logits $\mathbf{l}_{seg} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times M}$. For binary segmentation ($M=1$), $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{seg} = \sigma(\mathbf{l}_{seg})$ with $\sigma(\cdot)$ the sigmoid; for $M > 1$, class probabilities are obtained via channel-wise Softmax.

The joint multi-task training objective is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{total}(\theta, \phi, \mathbf{W}_{cls}) = \lambda_{cls} \mathcal{L}_{cls}(\mathbf{y}_{cls}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{cls}) + \lambda_{seg} \mathcal{L}_{seg}(\mathbf{y}_{seg}, \mathbf{l}_{seg}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{cls}$ is categorical cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$. To address class imbalance, the classification loss incorporates inverse-frequency balanced class weights $w_c = N / (K \cdot N_c)$~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}, matching the weighting formulation specified in Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask} and implemented in our training pipeline. The segmentation loss is purely cross-entropy without an active Dice-based gradient penalty:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{seg}(\mathbf{y}_{seg}, \mathbf{l}_{seg}) = 
    \begin{cases}
        \mathcal{L}_{BCE}(\mathbf{y}_{seg}, \mathbf{l}_{seg}), & M = 1, \\
        \mathcal{L}_{CE}(\mathbf{y}_{seg}, \mathbf{l}_{seg}), & M > 1,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
evaluating binary cross-entropy on binary tasks (TCGA, SIIM) and multi-class cross-entropy on multi-class tasks (PANDA, PanNuke). Rhanoui et al. formulated segmentation as a binary cancer-vs-background detection task across all datasets; our audit matches this binary objective for TCGA and SIIM, while evaluating multi-class cross-entropy for PANDA and PanNuke to directly benchmark their multi-category annotations. Both pipelines incorporate standard spatial data augmentations (horizontal and vertical flips, random rotation $\pm 15^\circ$, and affine shear $\pm 10^\circ$).

The Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) is evaluated strictly post-hoc as a validation metric, not as an active loss term during training. While cross-entropy minimization can in principle yield segmentations with high spatial overlap, evaluating Dice post-hoc on sparse and negative-dominant cohorts makes the metric vulnerable to evaluation conventions, as derived below.

\subsection{The Empty-Mask Dice Metric Degeneracy}\label{sec:dice}
The S{\o}rensen--Dice coefficient between ground-truth mask $Y$ and binary prediction $\hat{Y}$ ($Y, \hat{Y} \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$) is:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Dice}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{2 |Y \cap \hat{Y}|}{|Y| + |\hat{Y}| + \epsilon}, \quad \epsilon = 10^{-8}; \;\; \text{Dice} := 1 \;\text{if}\; |Y| + |\hat{Y}| = 0.
\end{equation}
Empty references and predictions represent a known validation trap in biomedical image analysis~\cite{reinke2024metrics}. Two common conventions govern empty slices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Foreground-Only Evaluation:} Compute Dice strictly over non-empty target slices ($\{i \mid |Y_i| > 0\}$).
    \item \textbf{Empty-Credit Assignment:} Assign $\text{Dice} = 1.0$ when $|Y| = 0 \land |\hat{Y}| = 0$, and $\text{Dice} = 0.0$ when $|Y| = 0 \land |\hat{Y}| > 0$.
\end{itemize}
Averaged across all $N$ slices under empty-credit assignment, the macroscopic mean is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice_dice}
    \overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{all}} = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} d_i, \;\; d_i = \text{Dice}(Y_i, \hat{Y}_i) \;\text{if}\; |Y_i| > 0, \;\text{else}\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[|\hat{Y}_i| = 0\right].
\end{equation}
Let $\rho = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}[|Y_i|=0]$ denote the proportion of ground-truth negative (lesion-free) slices, and let $q_{\text{empty}} = P(|\hat{Y}|=0 \mid |Y|=0)$ denote the model's true-negative prediction rate on negative slices. The exact macroscopic identity is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex_dice}
    \overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{all}} = \rho \cdot q_{\text{empty}} + (1 - \rho) \cdot \overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{fg}},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{fg}}$ is the mean Dice score over lesion-bearing slices ($|Y_i|>0$).

When all ground-truth negative images are predicted empty ($q_{\text{empty}} = 1$), the identity simplifies to $\overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{all}} = \rho + (1 - \rho) \cdot \overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{fg}}$. If a model produces false-positive segmentations on negative images, $q_{\text{empty}} < 1$. For a degenerate, completely collapsed predictor that emits empty masks on all inputs ($|\hat{Y}|=0$ everywhere), foreground Dice is zero ($\overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{fg}} = 0$) while $q_{\text{empty}} = 1$, yielding $\overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{all}} = \rho$.

Consequently, on sparse-lesion cohorts such as SIIM-ACR pneumothorax ($\rho = 77.72\%$), an all-empty baseline scores $77.72\%$ solely from empty-mask credit. Crucially, as shown by Eq.~\ref{eq:convex_dice}, reaching an overall score of $99\%$ on such a cohort requires positive-case foreground Dice of approximately $\overline{\text{Dice}}_{\text{fg}} \approx (0.99 - 0.7772)/(1 - 0.7772) \approx 95.51\%$ under perfect negative predictions ($q_{\text{empty}}=1$). Our SIIM runs demonstrate how overall Dice can conceal foreground failure: they emit all-empty masks on 19 of 20 folds, directly realizing the all-empty score floor ($77.72\%$ pooled across folds for Runs 05, 06, and 11; $77.04\%$ for Run 12 due to a small non-empty residual on fold 4; per-fold range $74.33\%-77.75\%$). While the definitive cause of the published 99\% Dice in Rhanoui et al. cannot be determined without access to their predictions, masks, and scoring scripts, this empirical collapse demonstrates that macroscopic Dice on sparse cohorts can register high scores despite zero foreground delineation. Fig.~\ref{fig:empty_dice} illustrates this degeneracy.

This metric inflation generalizes to multi-class histopathology. On 6-class PANDA prostate biopsies, individual tiles typically contain only one or two histological patterns, leaving 4--5 classes absent ($|Y_c|=0$). Under channel-wise empty crediting, an all-background model receives $\text{Dice}=1.0$ for every absent class alongside high agreement on background. Throughout this paper, we track two distinct estimators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Training-Time Batch Validation Macro-Dice:} Computed during training validation via batch-pooled channel-wise Dice (\texttt{dice\_coefficient}), crediting an empty channel across the batch with $1.0$, and averaging across all $M$ channels (fold-mean across 12 PANDA configurations: $82.77\%-84.39\%$).
    \item \emph{All-Slices Empty-Credit Pooled Per-Case Dice:} Pools all validation slices across folds, credits empty-over-empty cases with $\text{Dice}=1.0$, and averages per case (yielding the $98.54\%-98.60\%$ floor on PANDA, despite near-zero true glandular delineation; Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Adaptive Gradient Balancing via GradNorm}\label{sec:gnorm}
To test whether dynamic loss balancing mitigates cross-task gradient competition, we introduced a parameterized variant of \textsc{GradNorm}~\cite{chen2018gradnorm} in Phase~v2.

Let $W$ denote the shared convolutional filters and normalization layers of the U-Net encoder $f_\theta$. The $L_2$ gradient norm of task $i \in \{seg, cls\}$ with respect to $W$ at minibatch $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
    G_W^{(i)}(t) = \left\| \nabla_{W} w_i(t) \mathcal{L}_i(t) \right\|_2.
\end{equation}
For stability in the primary \texttt{Adam} optimizer, balancing weights are learnable log-weights $\mu_i(t) = \ln w_i(t)$ (initialized to $w_{seg}(0) = 5.0$, $w_{cls}(0) = 1.0$), updated jointly with the network parameters. Minibatch optimization minimizes the $L_1$ gradient disparity objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{grad}(t) = \sum_{i \in \{seg, cls\}} \left| \, G_W^{(i)}(t) \;-\; \bar{G}_W(t) \cdot [r_i(t)]^{\alpha} \right|,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{G}_W(t) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_j G_W^{(j)}(t)$ is the average gradient norm across tasks, $\alpha = 1.5$ is the asymmetry parameter, and $r_i(t)$ represents the relative inverse training rate:
\begin{equation}
    r_i(t) = \frac{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_i(t)}{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j \in \{seg, cls\}} \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_j(t)}, \quad \text{with} \quad \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_i(t) = \frac{\mathcal{L}_i(t)}{\mathcal{L}_i(0)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_i(0)$ is the initial step-0 loss. Following Chen et al.~\cite{chen2018gradnorm}, slower-training tasks exhibit higher loss ratios $r_i(t) > 1$, which raises their target gradient norm $\bar{G}_W(t) [r_i(t)]^\alpha$. After each optimizer update, we renormalize the weights so that $\sum_i w_i(t) = 2$. In the canonical formulation~\cite{chen2018gradnorm}, the module differentiates $\mathcal{L}_{grad}$ against detached targets and applies it to the balancing weights alone through a dedicated optimizer (learning rate $0.025$). \S\ref{sec:gn} probes this distinction empirically. We evaluate the module across Groups 2 and 4 to isolate how dynamic gradient equalization behaves in hard-parameter-sharing architectures.

\subsection{Color Deconvolution and Optical Density Space}\label{sec:macenko}
To mitigate staining variability across clinical laboratories, the v2 pipeline incorporates Macenko stain normalization~\cite{macenko2009method}. We convert RGB pixel intensities $\mathbf{I} \in [0, 255]^3$ to optical density (OD) space:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{OD} = -\ln\left(\frac{\mathbf{I} + \epsilon}{I_0}\right),
\end{equation}
where $I_0 = 255$ represents transmitted light intensity. The implementation computes optical density via natural logarithms $-\ln(\cdot)$ rather than the canonical base-10 formulation $-\log_{10}(\cdot)$~\cite{macenko2009method}. We estimate the hematoxylin and eosin stain vectors by eigendecomposition of the optical-density covariance matrix (stain-vector angles restricted to the 1st--99th percentile range) over OD values above a threshold of $0.15$, and project them onto a target reference vector. This step effectively eliminates macroscopic batch variations. However, projecting raw RGB channels onto static stain vectors alters chromatic contrast and stain concentration profiles across the tile, potentially attenuating subtle chromatin textures and colour variations that contribute to distinguishing fine glandular structures (Fig.~\ref{fig:macenko}).

We evaluated four benchmark datasets spanning diverse anatomical modalities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{TCGA-LGG Brain Tumor:} 2-class lesion classification (no tumor vs.\ tumor) and FLAIR abnormality segmentation on 2D $256 \times 256$ MRI slices ($N=3,929$; 110 patients~\cite{buda2019lgg}; strictly partitioned via patient-level \texttt{GroupKFold} isolation~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}).
    \item \textbf{PANDA Prostate Biopsies:} Clinical 6-class ordinal ISUP Gleason grading (grades 0--5) and gland segmentation ($N=10,516$ tiles~\cite{bulten2022artificial}; 5-fold cross-validation). In the baseline study of Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask}, the reported 6 classes were morphological tissue categories from Radboud segmentation masks (Background, Stroma, Benign epithelium, Gleason 3, 4, 5; Table 2 of~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask}), where non-cancerous background and stroma alone constituted $52.3\%$ of the dataset ($5,558$ of $10,618$ images). Moreover, Karolinska biopsies in PANDA lack these granular categories. Our experiments evaluate clinical 6-class ordinal ISUP grading; because these represent distinct classification targets, their accuracies are not directly comparable. Under the tested CNN architectures on $128 \times 128$ thumbnails, clinical ISUP grading achieves $28.21\%-44.87\%$ top-1 accuracy.
    \item \textbf{SIIM-ACR Pneumothorax:} Binary chest-radiography detection and pleural boundary segmentation ($N=10,675$ images~\cite{siim2019pneumothorax}; because the public release contains exactly one image per patient, sample-level grouping coincides with patient-level grouping, yielding a stratified 5-fold partition over image-level labels).
    \item \textbf{PanNuke (Multi-Organ Benchmark):} 19-tissue multi-organ histopathology benchmark ($N=7,901$ patches~\cite{gamper2020pannuke}; image-level 5-fold split). The baseline study by Rhanoui et al. reported joint multi-task performance across dermoscopic images from ISIC 2018~\cite{codella2019isic}. However, in the official ISIC 2018 release, Task 1 provided lesion segmentation masks for $2,594$ images, whereas Task 3 provided disease classification labels for $10,015$ images. Because Rhanoui et al. released no split manifests, code, or explanation of how joint multi-task training was conducted across cohorts with differing annotation availability (e.g., whether unannotated images were masked or omitted), their experimental protocol cannot be reconstructed. We therefore evaluated PanNuke, which provides fully paired, verified 19-class tissue labels and 6-class cell segmentation masks across diverse organs.
\end{enumerate}

We trained all models with PyTorch (up to 50 epochs with early stopping, patience 10; batch size 32; fixed seed 42 per fold) using ImageNet-pretrained encoders and the \texttt{Adam} optimizer with a constant per-phase learning rate and zero weight decay, and we applied no learning-rate schedule or additional regularization~\cite{davila2024finetuning}. Training ran under bf16 autocast AMP with PyTorch's \texttt{GradScaler}~\cite{pytorchamp}, with the scaler's \texttt{unscale\_()} called before gradient-norm clipping (max-norm $1.0$) at every optimizer step~\cite{moassefi2024checklist,maleki2024ridge}, and the \textsc{GradNorm} log-weights bounded to $[\pm\log 10]$ (capping each task weight at $10$). The true pre-clip gradient norm is logged per epoch, and clipping engagement is dataset-dependent: final-10-epoch per-fold mean engagement rates are $0.285$ (TCGA, VGG16), $0.516$ (PanNuke, no Macenko), $0.879$ (PanNuke, VGG16), $0.943$ (SIIM, VGG16), and $0.999$ (PANDA) --- on three datasets (PanNuke VGG16, SIIM, PANDA) clipping is an active, frequently-binding constraint, not a dormant safeguard. Non-finite gradients triggered $52$ bounded single-batch skips across the $135$ fold-cells, overwhelmingly isolated events (the $25$-skip-per-fold cap was never reached); each skipped batch is excluded from epoch metrics and stamped in the fold summaries. One fold (PanNuke, VGG16, final, fold 1) hit a non-finite forward loss at epoch 46; the guard aborted it loudly, and training resumed from the epoch-45 checkpoint, completing epochs 46--50 with zero skips --- a transient bf16 overflow, disclosed here as a resumed fold. \texttt{torch.compile} (\texttt{max-autotune}) was used only in the Phase~v1 replication arms by design; Phase~v2 arms run eager, and one Phase~v1 fold degraded to eager after a Triton capture failure (logged). Mid-campaign the resume/identity machinery was hardened (checkpoint fingerprints, atomic state writes); all $135$ fold-cells of record were produced under the fixed regime and re-verified from the fold summaries. In Phase~v1 (Runs 01--06), we replicated the original static setup ($\eta=10^{-3}$, $\lambda_{seg}:\lambda_{cls}=5:1$, raw inputs). In Phase~v2 (Runs 07--12), we incorporated dynamic \textsc{GradNorm} ($\alpha=1.5$, $\eta=10^{-4}$) and Macenko stain normalization~\cite{macenko2009method}. Groups 3--5 (Runs 13--26) systematically evaluate multi-organ generalizability, optimization hyperparameter isolation, and architectural skip ablations. Accuracy is reported as the five-fold mean; 95\% CIs are fold-level bootstrap percentiles ($10{,}000$ resamples of the five fold accuracies with replacement, seed 42), and Dice CIs are the across-folds per-case bootstrap intervals tabulated in the released matrix. Table~\ref{tab:results_matrix} summarizes the campaign per dataset and Figure~\ref{fig:results_dotplot} plots every run.

\section{Discussion \& Methodological Anatomy}

\subsection{The Anatomy of the PANDA Collapse}\label{sec:panda}
The largest gap in Table~\ref{tab:results_matrix} sits on the PANDA prostate biopsy benchmark. The original authors reported $88.0\%$ accuracy on 6-class grading with a $99.0\%$ Dice score. Our 12 PANDA configurations tell a very different story: fold-mean top-1 accuracy spans $28.21\%-44.87\%$ (95\% bootstrap CIs $[24.76, 31.47]$ to $[43.93, 45.76]$) and the all-slices empty-credit Dice floor is $98.54\%-98.60\%$ (per-epoch validation macro-Dice $82.77\%-84.39\%$). The static $5{:}1$-weighted $10^{-3}$ baselines (Runs 03--04) reach $34.51\%$ and $39.91\%$ accuracy; Run 17 (the isolated-learning-rate arm at $\eta=10^{-4}$, $44.87\%$) represents the strongest configuration in our campaign.

This performance difference reflects distinct target definitions. As documented in Table 2 of Rhanoui et al.~\cite{rhanoui2025multitask}, the cited study evaluated 6 morphological tissue classes extracted from Radboud segmentation masks (Background, Stroma, Benign epithelium, Gleason 3, 4, 5), where non-cancerous background glass and benign stroma comprised over $52\%$ of the samples ($5,558$ of $10,618$ images). In contrast, our audit evaluates authentic clinical 6-class ordinal ISUP grading (grades 0--5), which requires resolving architectural glandular patterns across the biopsy core~\cite{bulten2022artificial}. The cited study's $88\%$ accuracy and our $28.21\%-44.87\%$ accuracy evaluate different classification tasks and are therefore not directly comparable. In our experiments on $128 \times 128$ thumbnails with standard 2D CNNs, ISUP grading yields $28.21\%-44.87\%$, highlighting the challenge of ordinal grading under low-resolution inputs without multiscale attention or multiple-instance learning~\cite{bulten2022artificial}. Figure~\ref{fig:panda_anatomy} traces the training dynamics and isolates the optimization mechanisms involved.

\subsection{Controlled Skip Connection Ablation}\label{sec:skip}
We evaluate lateral skip connections in matched pairs, holding learning rate, GradNorm, and Macenko normalization identical:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coarse macro-tumor structures (TCGA):} Run 25 (skip-ablated) vs.\ matched baseline Run 08 (both $\eta=10^{-4}$, GradNorm, Macenko): accuracy $95.28\%$ vs.\ $95.26\%$ ($\Delta_{\text{Acc}} = +0.02$ percentage points), Dice $86.16\%$ vs $89.22\%$ ($\Delta_{\text{Dice}} = -3.06$ in mean fold validation macro-Dice; across-folds per-case bootstrap Dice: $85.42\%$ [85.11, 85.73] vs.\ $88.85\%$ [88.58, 89.12]).
    \item \textbf{Fine micro-glandular structures (PANDA):} Run 26 (skip-ablated) vs.\ matched baseline Run 10: accuracy $37.44\%$ vs.\ $37.56\%$ ($\Delta_{\text{Acc}} = -0.12$ percentage points), Dice $84.29\%$ vs $82.77\%$ ($\Delta_{\text{Dice}} = +1.52$ in mean fold validation macro-Dice; across-folds per-case bootstrap Dice: $98.54\%$ [98.36, 98.68] vs.\ $98.54\%$ [98.38, 98.67]).
\end{itemize}
The observed classification differences are $+0.02$ percentage points on TCGA and $-0.12$ on PANDA, and the marginal 95\% bootstrap CIs across the five folds overlap substantially (TCGA: $[94.14, 96.28]$ vs $[94.24, 96.39]$; PANDA: $[36.73, 37.99]$ vs $[37.20, 37.94]$). While this descriptive interval overlap indicates no large separation between the paired configurations, this analysis does not establish equivalence or formally test the significance of the paired differences. The fold-level bootstrap characterizes variability across the five cross-validation partitions rather than uncertainty over repeated independent training campaigns.

\paragraph{Macenko stain-normalization ablation.}
The matched Macenko pair (Group 5, Runs 23--24) exhibits an observable classification shift under matched settings. Omitting normalization raises PANDA top-1 accuracy by $+3.81$ percentage points (Run 23: $41.38\%$ vs.\ Run 10: $37.56\%$) and PanNuke accuracy by $+2.75$ percentage points (Run 24: $99.24\%$ vs.\ Run 16: $96.49\%$) with non-overlapping marginal 95\% bootstrap CIs across the five folds ($[39.76, 43.38]$ vs $[37.20, 37.94]$; $[99.11, 99.37]$ vs $[96.18, 96.90]$). Segmentation Dice also shifts: on PANDA, mean fold validation macro-Dice is $84.39\%$ vs.\ $82.77\%$ ($\Delta=+1.62$; across-folds per-case bootstrap Dice: $98.54\%$ vs.\ $98.54\%$); on PanNuke, mean fold validation macro-Dice is $70.76\%$ vs.\ $62.25\%$ ($\Delta=+8.51$; across-folds per-case bootstrap Dice: $77.61\%$ [77.10, 78.12] vs.\ $70.64\%$ [69.96, 71.32], $\Delta=+6.97$). Normalization altered the image color representation and was associated with lower classification accuracy in these matched experiments; the exact histological mechanism remains undetermined. Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_forest} consolidates all six matched pairs; Figure~\ref{fig:macenko} visualizes the chromatic contrast alteration.

\subsection{Isolating Learning Rate from Dynamic Gradient Balancing}\label{sec:gn}
Group 4 (PANDA $\times$ VGG16) factorizes the key optimization hyperparameters: learning rate ($\eta \in \{10^{-3}, 10^{-4}\}$), GradNorm-style balancing (on/off), and static loss-weighting ratios $\lambda_{seg}:\lambda_{cls} \in \{1{:}10, 1{:}1, 5{:}1, 10{:}1\}$, all under raw inputs.

\paragraph{Direct Isolation of Dynamic Gradient Balancing (Run 18).}
The isolated GradNorm arm at $\eta=10^{-3}$ under matched raw-input conditions (Run 18: PANDA $\times$ VGG16, GradNorm $\alpha=1.5$, raw input) compares directly against the static 5:1 baseline (Run 20). Under these matched conditions, activating GradNorm ($\alpha=1.5$) resulted in a validation accuracy decrease from $38.18\%$ to $28.21\%$ (95\% bootstrap CI across the five folds: $[24.76, 31.47]$ vs $[32.75, 41.79]$, $\Delta_{\text{Acc}} = -9.97$ percentage points), while fold validation macro-Dice remained identical at $84.39\%$.

This $28.21\%$ accuracy mirrors the lower accuracy observed in Phase v2 at $\eta=10^{-4}$ (Run 09: $33.34\%$ Acc). We hypothesize that dynamic gradient balancing in this configuration induces destructive gradient interference on the shared encoder, consistent with observations in multi-task optimization studies~\cite{chen2018gradnorm,standley2020tasks}. However, gradient directional alignment was not directly measured during training, and isolating whether the decrease stems specifically from the $\alpha=1.5$ asymmetry parameter would require a parametric sweep over $\alpha$ under identical matched conditions.

\paragraph{Learning Rate and Loss Weighting Dynamics.}
Holding GradNorm off, reducing the learning rate from $\eta=10^{-3}$ (Run 20: $38.18\%$ Acc) to $\eta=10^{-4}$ (Run 17: $44.87\%$) yields a statistically resolvable improvement, with disjoint 95\% bootstrap CIs across the five folds. The static loss-ratio sweep (Runs 19--22) confirms $\lambda=5{:}1$ and $10{:}1$ as comparable static balances ($36.51\%$--$39.71\%$), yet no ratio approaches the claimed $87\%-88\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lambda_sweep}). The isolated GradNorm arm (Run 18) drops to $28.21\%$ against the $38.18\%$ static baseline with non-overlapping CIs, and the canonical decoupled scheme plateaus at $22.70\%$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[14.66, 30.73]$). Dynamic balancing in either formulation cannot bridge the gap (Figures~\ref{fig:panda_anatomy}, \ref{fig:ablation_forest}).

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
Several methodological limitations of this audit should be explicitly considered:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Reconstruction of Underspecified Protocols:} Because the baseline authors released no training code, seed policies, or split manifests, our reproduction reconstructed the pipeline faithfully from published text descriptions and official benchmark repositories. Where published protocols could not be reconstructed due to unaligned benchmark releases (such as the discrepancy between ISIC 2018 Task 1 with $2,594$ segmentation masks and Task 3 with $10,015$ classification labels without cohort split manifests), we evaluated the established multi-tissue PanNuke benchmark instead.
    \item \emph{Thumbnail vs.\ Whole-Slide Multiscale Analysis:} Our audit directly tested standard 2D multi-task U-Nets on fixed-resolution tiles as specified in the published recipe. While necessary to evaluate the claims directly, thumbnail-level classification cannot capture the multiscale context required for gigapixel whole-slide grading in computational pathology.
    \item \emph{Computational Budget and Seed Scope:} Each of the 27 configurations was evaluated across 5-fold cross-validation under fixed seeds per fold (135 total fold-runs) with an early-stopping patience of 10 epochs. While fold-level bootstrap confidence intervals characterize data-partitioning uncertainty, evaluating multiple random seeds per fold across all 135 models was computationally prohibitive.
    \item \emph{Metric Estimator Sensitivity:} As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:dice}, biomedical segmentation metrics depend heavily on aggregation conventions. We disclosed both batch-pooled validation macro-Dice and per-case empty-credit Dice to make these dependencies transparent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion \& Recommendations}
Across 27 configurations $\times$ 5 folds (135 fold-cells), reported near-saturated metrics ($88\%-99\%$) did not reproduce under rigorous cross-validation. The performance gap stems from dataset complexity, task-formulation divergence, and multi-task optimization trade-offs: lateral skip connections exhibit modest paired differences ($+0.02$ to $-0.12$ pts), while dynamic gradient balancing ($\alpha=1.5$) is associated with lower validation accuracy ($38.18\% \to 28.21\%$ Acc). We recommend: (1) mandatory reporting of sample and patient grouping cardinalities, ensuring independent partition units; (2) explicit mathematical definition of the Dice evaluation convention, reporting foreground-only lesion overlap on sparse datasets alongside negative-case false-positive rates; (3) reporting 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals and descriptive paired comparisons on matched ablations to distinguish architectural effects from fold variance; (4) validating pathology models on aligned clinical tasks and multiscale benchmarks rather than low-resolution proxy thumbnails; and (5) systematic evaluation of multi-task balancing hyperparameters before clinical translation.

None of the mechanisms quantified here is exotic. Each is grounded in the evaluation literature (Section~\ref{sec:related}), and the study whose figures motivated this audit is already being cited as evidence of multi-task efficacy in follow-on oncology work. The recommendations above are offered as a verification checklist for multi-task detection and segmentation models intended for clinical consumption.
